Before you write a single prompt, choose your aspect ratio. It affects composition, camera movement and where your video can be published. Changing it later usually means cropping or regenerating.

## 16:9, the widescreen standard

Use 16:9 for YouTube videos, websites, presentations and anything watched on a computer or TV. It suits wide landscapes, group scenes and horizontal pans.

> Slow pan from left to right across the valley, mist rising from the river, soft morning light.

## 9:16, vertical for phones

Use 9:16 for Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts and TikTok. Vertical frames favor tall subjects: portraits, buildings, waterfalls and full-body shots. Tilts and rises feel natural here.

> Slow upward tilt along the tower, clouds moving behind it, late afternoon sun.

## 1:1, the square

Use 1:1 for feed posts, product listings and ads where space is shared with text. It works well for centered subjects such as products, food and logos.

> Smooth orbit around the sneaker, soft reflections on the surface, clean white background.

## Match the image to the ratio

The closer your source image is to the target ratio, the better the result. If you start with a horizontal photo and ask for 9:16, the tool has to invent new areas at the top and bottom, which can cause odd details.

- Crop or recompose the image first when possible.
- Keep the main subject in the center when you may crop later.
- Avoid large camera moves if the tool must extend the image.

## Quick chooser

- **YouTube or website:** 16:9
- **Reels, Shorts, TikTok:** 9:16
- **Feed posts and product ads:** 1:1

Image-to-Video Prompt Examples for Beginners

By the Image2Motion AI team · Updated October 17, 2026

Image-to-video AI tools take a single picture and animate it into a short clip. The picture does most of the work. Your prompt only has to answer one question: what should move, and how? This guide shows you how to write that prompt and gives you ten examples you can adapt right away.

What makes a good image-to-video prompt?

With text-to-video you describe everything. With image-to-video the image is already the description, so a short, focused prompt usually wins. A good beginner prompt has four ingredients:

  • Motion: what moves (hair, water, clouds, the camera).
  • Camera: push-in, pan, orbit or static.
  • Speed and mood: slow, gentle, dramatic, calm.
  • Protection: what must stay the same.

A simple template

[Camera move], [subject motion], [environment motion], [light or mood], [keep X consistent].

10 prompt examples

1. Portrait

Slow push-in, the woman blinks and smiles slightly, hair moving gently in the wind, soft natural light, keep her face consistent.

2. Landscape

Static camera, clouds drifting across the sky, grass swaying in the wind, golden sunset light, calm and peaceful mood.

3. Food

Slow orbit around the burger, steam rising, sauce glistening, warm restaurant lighting, keep the food shape unchanged.

4. Product

Smooth rotation around the sneaker, soft light reflections moving across the surface, clean white background, logo stays sharp.

5. City at night

Slow rise above the street, car lights streaking, neon signs flickering softly, light mist, cinematic atmosphere.

6. Animal

Subtle handheld camera, the dog turns its head and wags its tail, fur moving slightly, bright outdoor daylight.

7. Fantasy illustration

Gentle parallax drift, glowing particles floating around the castle, flags waving, clouds rolling slowly, magical soft glow.

8. Ocean

Slow pan along the shoreline, waves rolling in and foam spreading on the sand, seagulls gliding in the distance, soft dawn colors.

9. Car

Low-angle tracking shot beside the car, subtle dust on the road, reflections sliding along the body, dramatic sunset sky, car design unchanged.

10. News or poster style graphic

Slow push-in on the central scene, smoke and light flickering subtly, small camera shake, keep all text and layout sharp and readable.

Do and don't

DoDon't
Use one clear camera moveStack pan + zoom + tilt + orbit in one prompt
Add speed words: slow, gentle, subtleLeave speed unspecified
Name the elements that should moveWrite only "make it move"
Add a consistency instructionAssume faces and logos will stay perfect
Keep prompts to 20-45 wordsWrite long paragraphs that repeat the image

Troubleshooting

Faces or objects look distorted

Slow the motion down, reduce the number of moving parts and add "keep the face consistent" or "no morphing."

Nothing moves

Name specific moving elements such as hair, clouds, water, smoke or fabric, and add a gentle camera move.

The motion is too fast or chaotic

Add "slow," "subtle" or "gentle" and remove secondary effects.

Text in the image becomes unreadable

Keep the camera nearly static, avoid zooms, and add "keep text sharp and readable." Very small text may still change, so check the result carefully.

Build a longer video with shots

Most tools generate short clips. To make a 10 to 30 second video, plan several shots, generate each clip from its own image or frame, and join them in an editor. Our guide on camera movement prompts explains how to vary the camera between shots so the sequence feels edited.
